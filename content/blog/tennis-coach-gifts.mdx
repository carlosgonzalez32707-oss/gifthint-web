If you've ever stared at a search bar trying to figure out what to get someone who spends every waking hour on a tennis court, you're not alone.

Tennis coaches are notoriously difficult to shop for. They already have rackets. They definitely have balls. And the last thing they want is another "World's Best Coach" mug gathering dust in the staff room.

The good news: coaches are actually easy to shop for *if you know what to look for*. They spend hours in the sun, on their feet, teaching the same drills — and the things that make that grind more comfortable are deeply appreciated.

Here are 15 ideas across a range of budgets, from practical to genuinely personal.

## Practical Gear for the Court

### 1. A Proper Tennis Hopper Basket

Every coach spends a chunk of their day collecting balls. A large hopper basket with a stand — the kind that lets you pick up balls without bending — is something coaches rarely buy themselves but immediately love having. Look for at least 70-ball capacity and a folding stand. Tourna, Gamma, and Prince all make solid options under $60.

### 2. High-SPF Cooling Towel

Coaches are in the sun for four to eight hours on busy days. A UPF-rated, quick-dry cooling towel is practical and used every single day. Get one with a snap loop so it clips directly to a bag — coaches lose towels constantly.

### 3. Polarised Sport Sunglasses

Off-the-shelf sunglasses don't work for coaches who need to track balls against a bright sky. Oakley, Maui Jim, and Bollé make polarised sport frames designed for exactly this — lenses that let you see a tennis ball in flight without squinting. Budget $80–$200 for a pair worth giving.

### 4. Court Shoes (Their Exact Brand)

If you know their brand — ASICS, Nike Court, New Balance 996 — a new pair of court shoes is one of the most appreciated gifts you can give. Court shoes wear out fast and coaches perpetually delay replacing them. If you don't know their specific model, a gift card to a specialist sports retailer is a safer choice.

## Tech and Training Tools

### 5. A Swing Analysis App Subscription

Swing Vision (iOS) and Zepp Tennis use the phone camera or a wearable sensor to analyse stroke mechanics in real time. A one-year subscription gives a coach objective data to share with players — and to improve their own game. Thoughtful and genuinely useful.

### 6. A Portable Rebounder

For coaches who travel to clubs or run camps, a portable rebounder lets them set up drills anywhere. Gamma and Lobster make compact options under $120 that fold into a carry bag. The kind of purchase coaches think about but rarely make for themselves.

### 7. Ball Machine Session Credits

Many facilities rent ball machines by the hour. A multi-session credit at their home club lets a coach run private drills without paying out of pocket. Call the club directly to arrange a gift certificate — most are happy to do this.

## Recovery and Wellness

### 8. A Percussion Massager

Standing on hard courts destroys calves, knees, and feet. The Theragun Mini is compact enough to live in a tennis bag and powerful enough to matter. Theragun and Hypervolt are the two brands worth buying. Anything under $50 tends to underdeliver on durability.

### 9. Compression Socks (Multiple Pairs)

Unglamorous, genuinely appreciated. Compression socks reduce foot fatigue on long court days and coaches go through them quickly. Swiftwick and CEP make court-specific options. Buy a multi-pack — it essentially doubles the gift for the same price.

### 10. Quality Insoles for Court Shoes

A two-pack of Superfeet or Currex sport insoles costs under $50, fits in a card, and provides immediate relief. Coaches replace these less frequently than they should. A multi-pack here, too, if you want to pad the gift.

## Personal and Memorable

### 11. A Good Coffee Shop Gift Card

Coaches drink a lot of coffee. This is universal. A generous gift card to a café near their facility is genuinely useful — and won't require you to know anything about their preferences.

### 12. A Monogrammed Towel or Personalised Bag

A personalised towel or tennis bag from Etsy — embroidered with their initials or club name — feels thoughtful without requiring exact knowledge of their preferences. Many sellers can add a club logo. Search "embroidered sports towel" on Etsy and browse from there.

### 13. A Quality Water Bottle

Coaches are perpetually dehydrated and perpetually forgetting their bottles. A Stanley Quencher or Owala FreeSip in a colour they wouldn't choose for themselves makes a solid gift. If the retailer offers engraving, adding their name makes it unmistakably theirs.

### 14. A Lesson Planner or Coaching Journal

Some coaches are methodical about tracking student progress. A quality A5 notebook from Leuchtturm1917 or a dedicated coaching journal helps them structure their notes season-over-season. It signals you see the preparation work that happens off the court.

### 15. An Experience Far From a Tennis Court

This is the gift that requires the most thought — and tends to be remembered longest. A cooking class. A spa afternoon. Tickets to a professional match they'd watch as a fan, not an analyst. The gifts that pull a coach *away* from their work, briefly, are the ones that make them feel genuinely seen.
